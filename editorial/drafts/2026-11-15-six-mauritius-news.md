## 1. Port-Louis: revised traffic diversions to begin 16 November

**Category:** Transport  
**Status:** Draft — editorial review required

The authorities plan to introduce revised traffic diversions around Monseigneur Leen Street in Port-Louis from Monday 16 November 2026, following congestion caused by emergency road and retaining-wall repairs. The Ministry of Land Transport previously announced a partial closure between G.E.F. Poulin Street and D’Alembert Street from 6 November to 21 December. Drivers have been directed towards Old Moka Road and alternative routes. The precise revised arrangements should be checked against the ministry's forthcoming notice before journeys. Motorists are advised to follow temporary signs and exercise caution near the worksite.

## 2. Bank of Mauritius raises Key Rate to 5%

**Category:** Economy  
**Status:** Draft — editorial review required

The Monetary Policy Committee of the Bank of Mauritius unanimously increased the Key Rate by 25 basis points, from 4.75% to 5.00%, on 11 November 2026. The bank cited broadening inflation pressures and risks to price stability. It reported that year-on-year inflation reached 6.4% in October, while projecting economic growth of 3.0% for 2026. The decision could increase borrowing costs, although the effect on individual loans depends on banks' pricing and contractual terms. The bank said it would continue monitoring economic developments.

## 3. Ramgoolam to brief Parliament on Chagos; water and youth dialogue also priorities

**Category:** Politics  
**Status:** Draft — editorial review required

Prime Minister Navin Ramgoolam returned to Mauritius following international engagements including the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting and the Paris Peace Forum. According to reports citing a Prime Minister's Office statement dated 14 November, he is expected to address the National Assembly on Tuesday 17 November about the Chagos dossier. The government also intends to continue consultations with young people and review water-supply difficulties with the relevant authorities. The outcome of the planned parliamentary briefing and meetings has not yet been announced.

## 4. Mare-Chicose: leachate transport disruption raises concerns

**Category:** Environment  
**Status:** Draft — editorial review required

Transporters responsible for removing leachate from the Mare-Chicose landfill reportedly suspended operations for about a week over unpaid amounts, according to Défi Média on 14 November. Environment Minister Rajesh Bhagwan acknowledged the payment issue and expressed dissatisfaction with aspects of the site's management. Joanna Bérenger raised concerns about retention-basin capacity and possible overflow during heavy rainfall. The minister, however, said there was no immediate ecological disaster risk and reported that the contractor intended to resume pumping on 14 November. The resumption and current basin levels require further confirmation.

## 5. Health Minister announces recruitment of 60 nurses from India

**Category:** Health  
**Status:** Draft — editorial review required

Health Minister Anil Bachoo announced plans to bring in 60 nurses from India to reinforce hospital teams facing staff shortages, according to Défi Média's report on 14 November. Speaking after a workshop in Quatre-Bornes about national diabetes guidelines, the minister also discussed screening and prevention initiatives. The report quotes him as saying nearly 20% of Mauritians have diabetes and 15% are prediabetic. Recruitment timing, deployment details and the underlying prevalence estimates should be confirmed through subsequent official releases.

## 6. Rs 347 million allocated to coastal rehabilitation across 17 sites

**Category:** Environment  
**Status:** Draft — editorial review required

The government has allocated Rs 347 million from the Climate and Sustainability Fund for coastal protection work covering approximately 16.5 kilometres across 17 priority sites, according to reporting on the Cabinet meeting of 13 November. Planned interventions may combine engineering works with nature-based measures such as mangrove planting and restoration of native dune vegetation. Three consulting firms have reportedly been engaged for assessments, recommendations, tender preparation and supervision. A complete site-by-site schedule and budget breakdown have not yet been published in the reviewed reports.

**Sources:** [Source](https://ionnews.mu/rehabilitation-cotiere-rs-347-millions-allouees-pour-proteger-17-sites-prioritaires/)
